DAW manual · Live · Getting started

1 What the DAW builds do

The DAW builds turn the SMK-37 into a controller that knows your DAW. With the Oscuro script in Bitwig Studio, Ableton Live or FL Studio, the knobs follow the device you are working on, the faders mix, the pads select and mute tracks, and the screen shows names, values, levels and the browser.

One build: DAW BT

BuildFileWhat it is
DAW BToscuro-<version>-daw_bt-app.binThe DAW build: USB MIDI and the DAW link, plus Bluetooth LE MIDI, so the keyboard can also work without a cable to the computer.

Earlier versions also came as a USB-only DAW build with the same features. It is no longer published: DAW BT does everything it did over USB, and Bluetooth stays off until you tap BT. Everything in this manual works in DAW BT, except where a page says it needs Bluetooth.

The DAW builds run on the M-Vave SMK-37 Elite and its twins: the SMK-37 Pro, the MKE-P37 and the Starrykey 37 Play. Only the Elite and the Pro have been tried on a unit.

What the keyboard does in a DAW build

  • Keys, pads, wheels and pedal play MIDI into your DAW, as on any controller. The sound comes from the DAW. The build has no synth patches; a small built-in test voice plays from the keys and pads on the keyboard itself and cannot be switched off.
  • With the Oscuro script, the knobs, faders, pad bank B, Left / Right, SEQ, SEQ PLAY and SEQ REC belong to the DAW. The screen draws four views of your project: Device, Mixer, Overview and Browser.
  • The keyboard's own musical features stay: the Harmony Helper (a chord partner on the pads), Scale and Chord modes, the arpeggiator, Note Repeat and the step sequencer. Pad bank A stays notes, so you can play drums or chords while bank B controls tracks.
  • Without a script, or with M-Vave's stock script, the keyboard is a plain MIDI controller with editable presets.

How the pieces fit

Your computer runs the DAW and the Oscuro script for it. The script and the keyboard talk over the Oscuro DAW protocol: the keyboard reports what you touch ("K3 turned two steps"), the script decides what that does in the DAW and sends back what the screen and lights should show. Notes travel as ordinary MIDI beside it.

There are three scripts, one per DAW, and this manual comes in three books, one for each:

DAWScriptBook
Bitwig Studio 6.1 or newerSMK-37 Oscuro (controller extension)Oscuro for Bitwig Studio
Ableton Live 12 or newerSMK37_Oscuro (Remote Script)Oscuro for Ableton Live
FL Studio 2026SMK-37 Oscuro (MIDI script)Oscuro for FL Studio

The chapters about the keyboard itself are the same in every book; the setup, views, workflows and reference chapters are written for one DAW each.

A script for M-Vave's stock firmware is a different script; both can be installed side by side (see Set up Ableton Live).

Status. The DAW protocol and the three scripts are tested on a computer against a model of the DAW and the firmware's own decoder. The Ableton Live and FL Studio scripts have had only a short test with a keyboard; the Bitwig script has had the most use. Expect rough edges, and tell us what you find. Everything can be undone: the installer puts M-Vave's firmware back at any time.
