What the pads become is decided in this order: a Drum Rack, an open key lane, then these three, then device switches, then tracks.

Simpler in Slicing mode

The pads become the slices of the sample: sixteen at a time, lit in the track's colour, the selected slice bright, dark past the last slice.

  • Tap a pad to select that slice in Live. To hear it, play it on pad bank A: slice 1 is note 36 (C1), slice 2 note 37, and so on.
  • Oct- + Left / Right pages by sixteen slices. The status bar shows Slices 1-16/n.
  • The screen says Pads: <device> slices once.

Looper

The pads become the Looper's buttons. The pad of the Looper's current state is bright, the others dim.

PadDoes
1Record
2Overdub
3Play
4Stop
5Undo
6Clear (hold half a second; the screen says Looper cleared)
7Half speed
8Double speed
9Half length
10Double length

The screen says Pads: <device> looper once.

Racks with macro variations

An Instrument or Effect Rack with stored macro variations turns the pads into a variation recall board.

  • Top row: eight variations of a window. Existing ones are dim, the selected one bright. Tap to select and recall it.
  • Oct- + Left / Right moves the window by eight.
  • Bottom row: pad 9 Store (a new variation from the macros now), pad 10 Randomize the macros, pad 16 Delete the selected variation (red; hold half a second).
  • The screen says Pads: <rack> variations once.

A typical use: set the macros, Store; change them, Store again; then recall the variations from the pads while you play.

Good to know

  • Holding the Shift pad still shows the choice of track actions, and the Oct- / Shift pad chords keep their meaning.
  • Plain Left / Right stay the device banks.
  • These layouts are tested on a computer only, not yet in Live with a keyboard.
