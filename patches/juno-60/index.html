Make your own patches · JUNO-60

Make JUNO-60 patches with an AI chat

One file per synth, written for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any other AI chat. No tools, no Python: copy it, say what you want, load what you get back.

In the builds: Retrowave, Phosphor, Juno

What the AI can't do

The guide tells the AI to say these things at the end of its answer. Play the bank, tell the AI what to change, and load the new one.

End every answer that contains a bank with a short paragraph headed What I couldn't do, in your own words. It covers:

  • You cannot hear the sounds. The values are informed estimates from how the engine works, not tuned by ear; some patches will need adjusting on the keyboard.
  • Anything the player asked for that this engine cannot do, and what you made instead.
  • Values you had to guess, or patches you are least sure of.
  • Whether you built the file yourself, or the player has to paste your text into the installer. If the packer reported an error you could not fix, say which patch and what the error said.

Never claim that you listened to, played or tested the sounds.

For Juno-60 in particular: one oscillator with saw, pulse and sub; one envelope; no effects other than the chorus. Sounds that need FM, wavetables, several envelopes or reverb and delay are out of reach; make the nearest Juno-style sound and say so.

How it works (for the curious)

The tools and starter patches named here (tools/ and patches/) come with Oscuro's firmware packages. Each guide describes the firmware as of the version in the footer.

The Juno engine models a Juno-60/106 style voice: one DCO with saw, pulse and sub, noise, a resonant low-pass filter, one envelope, an LFO and the chorus. A patch is 48 bytes. You describe patches in a JSON file, os_bank_gen.py turns the file into a bank, and the installer sends the bank to the keyboard. You need os_bank_gen.py (in tools/) and Python 3. General matters (what a bank is, the patch memory, using an LLM) are in README.md next to this file. 512 ready-made patches are in patches/juno/.

The three steps

  1. Write my_juno.json (format below).
  2. Build the bank; the tool refuses unknown keys and out-of-range values:
python3 tools/os_bank_gen.py --engine jn --json my_juno.json --output my_juno.oscbank --bank-name "My Juno"
  1. Open the installer page, Synth patches, choose my_juno.oscbank, send. In the Retrowave and Phosphor builds the installer switches the keyboard to the engine of the file by itself; to switch by hand, hold Oct -, press PATCH, turn K1 to JUNO-60 and press >. The installer also takes Juno-106 SysEx dumps; a 106 patch maps onto the keys below.

The JSON file

A list of patch objects (or an object with a "patches" list), at most 128. Each has a name (plain ASCII, up to 10 characters) and any of the keys below. A key you leave out keeps the INIT value. cat and desc are notes for people.

[
  {"name": "WARM PAD", "P_CUTOFF": 62, "P_RES": 12, "P_ENV_MOD": 40, "P_ATTACK": 70, "P_RELEASE": 75,
   "P_SAW": 110, "P_PULSE": 70, "P_PWM": 50, "P_CHORUS": 2, "E_LFO_RATE": 30, "E_VCF_LFO_MOD": 20},
  {"name": "DEEP BASS", "P_CUTOFF": 40, "P_RES": 30, "P_ENV_MOD": 70, "P_ATTACK": 0, "P_DECAY": 30,
   "P_SAW": 120, "P_SUB": 110, "P_CHORUS": 0, "E_DCO_RANGE": 3}
]

Every key

All values are whole numbers. P_* are the 16 knob parameters (PARA and the second page), E_* are on the EDIT page.

KeyRangeINITWhat it does
P_CUTOFF0 to 12780Filter cutoff
P_RES0 to 12720Filter resonance
P_ENV_MOD0 to 12760How far the envelope opens the filter
P_ATTACK0 to 12710Envelope attack
P_DECAY0 to 12760Envelope decay
P_SUSTAIN0 to 12780Envelope sustain level
P_RELEASE0 to 12750Envelope release
P_LFO0 to 1270LFO to the pitch (vibrato), up to about a third of a semitone; the mod wheel adds to it
P_SAW0 to 127100Saw level
P_PULSE0 to 1270Pulse level
P_SUB0 to 12750Sub-oscillator level
P_NOISE0 to 1270Noise level
P_PWM0 to 12730Pulse width, or the PWM depth when the source is the LFO or the envelope
P_PORTA0 to 1270Portamento time
P_CHORUS0 to 620 off; 1 and 2 the Juno chorus (the two settings sound the same at present); 3 a fast variation; 4, 5, 6 a chorus panned left, centre, right
P_VOL0 to 127100Volume
E_DCO_RANGE1 to 42Octave range of the DCO: 1 is the highest, each step down is an octave lower (4 is three octaves below 1)
E_PWM_MOD_SRC0 to 21Pulse-width modulation source (0 manual, 1 LFO, 2 envelope)
E_VCA_TYPE0 to 10VCA follows the envelope (0) or is open as a gate (1)
E_LFO_RATE0 to 12764LFO rate
E_LFO_DELAY0 to 1270LFO delay (the LFO fades in after this time)
E_LFO_AUTO_TRIG0 to 11The LFO delay starts again with every note. Keep it at 1: with 0 the LFO can stay silent at present
E_VCF_LFO_MOD0 to 1270LFO to the cutoff
E_VCF_KEY_MOD0 to 1270Keyboard tracking of the cutoff
E_VCF_ENV_POL0 to 10Filter envelope polarity (1 inverts it)
E_HPF0 to 30High-pass filter step (0 off, 3 the highest)
E_CHORUS_DRYWET0 to 12764Chorus dry/wet mix
E_MODEL0 to 100 the Juno-60 behaviour, 1 the Juno-106
E_PORTA_MODE0 to 20Portamento: 0 off, 1 on every note, 2 only between overlapping (legato) notes
E_BEND_DCO0 to 12764With E_BEND_VCF, how far pitch bend moves the cutoff (64 = none). The pitch bend range itself is fixed at 2 semitones
E_BEND_VCF0 to 1270Pitch bend to the cutoff, centre 64; it works only with E_BEND_DCO away from 64

Recipes

  • Pad: cutoff 60 to 90, resonance 10 to 30, attack 40 to 80, release 50 to 80, saw plus pulse with some PWM, chorus on, an LFO on the filter.
  • Lead: cutoff 90 to 120, attack 0 to 10, release 30 to 50, saw or pulse dominant, portamento 10 to 30 for legato.
  • Bass: cutoff 30 to 60, resonance 20 to 50, attack 0 to 5, decay 20 to 40, sub at full, saw on, a low DCO range (3 or 4).
  • Strings: cutoff 70 to 90, attack 20 to 40, release 40 to 60, saw with a little pulse, PWM from the LFO, chorus II.

Asking an LLM

The simplest route is the one-file AI guide ../llm-guides/oscuro-jn-ai-guide.md: give it to an AI chat and get a bank back, no tools needed. With this guide instead, ask for the JSON only and build with the tool above.
