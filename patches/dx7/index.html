Make DX7 FM patches with an AI chat

One file per synth, written for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any other AI chat. No tools, no Python: copy it, say what you want, load what you get back.

In the builds: Retrowave, DX7

  1. Copy or download the guide. Download oscuro-fm-ai-guide.md
  2. Give it to an AI chat (paste it or attach the file) and say what you want, for example: “dark pads and basses for ambient techno”. The AI asks how many patches you want, then makes them.
  3. Get the bank back: a file, or a block of text if the AI cannot make files.
  4. Load it: in the installer's Synth patches tab, choose the file, or paste the text into “Paste text from the AI”. Then send it to the keyboard. Open the installer
What the AI can't do

The guide tells the AI to say these things at the end of its answer. Play the bank, tell the AI what to change, and load the new one.

End every answer that contains a bank with a short paragraph headed What I couldn't do, in your own words. It covers:

  • You cannot hear the sounds. The values are informed estimates from how the engine works, not tuned by ear; some voices will need adjusting on the keyboard.
  • Anything the player asked for that this engine cannot do, and what you made instead.
  • Values you had to guess, or voices you are least sure of.
  • Whether you built the file yourself, or the player has to paste your text into the installer. If the packer reported an error you could not fix, say which voice and what the error said.

Never claim that you listened to, played or tested the sounds.

For DX7 FM in particular: it is a six-operator FM synth with sine operators only (no filter, no samples, no effects inside the voice). Warm analog pads and resonant filter sweeps can only be imitated. A bank is one DX7 cartridge of 32 voices; when you make fewer, the rest of the cartridge is filled with plain INIT VOICE patches. Say so when it matters.

How it works (for the curious)

The tools and starter patches named here (tools/ and patches/) come with Oscuro's firmware packages. Each guide describes the firmware as of the version in the footer.

The DX7 FM engine is the DX7 core of Dexed (open source): it plays standard Yamaha DX7 voices (155-byte VCED, six operators, 12 notes at once), so patches sound as they do in Dexed. There is no JSON route and no starter bank here: DX7 patches are an enormous, well-established library, and the format is the standard one. General matters (what a bank is, the patch memory) are in README.md next to this file.

Where DX7 patches come from

  • Cartridge and bank files in the DX7 32-voice bulk dump format (.syx), including the thousands of banks that circulate for the DX7 and its clones (a bank is a cartridge: 32 voices).
  • Single-voice dumps (.syx) and MIDI files that contain them (.mid).
  • Any DX7 editor that can export SysEx: edit there, save the bank, bring it over.

Loading them

  1. Run the engine DX7 FM: it is the engine of the DX7 build; in the Retrowave build hold Oct -, press PATCH, turn K1 to DX7 FM, press >.
  2. Open the installer page, Synth patches, choose the .syx or .mid files and the Bank (16 banks of 32 patches, one DX7 cartridge each), and send.

The patch memory first needs the backup-and-erase step described in the user guide (Patch memory backup).

Editing on the keyboard

K1 to K8 offer macros that change the patch itself: Algo (algorithm 1 to 32), Feedbk, Mod Lvl, Car Lvl, Attack, Decay, Release, LFO; the second page has vibrato, transpose, bend range, portamento, mono, wheel, tune and volume. To save the changes, hold Oct - and press Right (Right again overwrites the stored patch, Left stores a copy).

Making new patches

FM programming is a craft of its own: start from a factory-like patch, change one operator at a time. Any DX7 patch editor works for designing, and the bank file it exports loads as above. A test package of the DX7 build carries patches/dx7/P8-test-bank.syx (a bank used in the budget tests), a convenient file for a first send.
